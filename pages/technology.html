In my spaAfter 26 years of running a spa on whatever software the vendor sold us, I spent a year in AI systems and built Opteva on GoHighLevel: the data model, the guardrails that block medical claims, the publishing. I have been on both sides of the demo. Steph: one systems project with a before and after, and the stack you actually implement and support (booking systems, phone, forms, payments, EMR you work alongside).

What the seat covers

  • Booking and CRM. One system of record for patients, appointments and follow-up. GoHighLevel first, since I build on it, but the right answer depends on what you already run.
  • Phones and missed-call follow-up. What happens when the front desk cannot answer. Usually nothing. That is the most expensive gap in the building.
  • Intake, consent and forms. Digital, signed before the appointment, filed where you can find them, including photo consent.
  • Automations between tools. Rebooking reminders, review requests, membership renewals, treatment-cycle campaigns, running without a person pressing a button.
  • AI tools, honestly evaluated. Which ones earn their subscription in a spa and which ones are a demo. I have built one; I know what the hard part is.
  • Reporting. The dashboard the finance and operations seats read, built from your actual systems.
  • Vendor decisions and contracts. When to switch, when not to, and what to never store where. Opteva was designed to hold zero protected health information; I apply the same discipline to your stack.

What you get each month

  • A monthly systems review: what broke, what is manual that should not be, what is next.
  • One automation or integration built or repaired each month, in priority order.
  • Vendor and renewal decisions made with you, with the trade-offs written down.
  • Documentation your team can follow when I am not in the room.
  • Steph: whether implementation is inside the seat or scoped separately, and anything else you commit to monthly.

How an engagement runs

  1. The audit first. Two to three weeks across all four areas, so the seat starts with a ranked list, not a guess.
  2. Thirty-day setup. Access, baselines, the first dashboard, the first fixes that pay for the month.
  3. The cadence. Steph: days or hours per month, remote or on site, how the month is structured.
  4. Term and price. Steph: minimum term and whether the price is public or scoped after the audit.

Who this is for

Owners with five subscriptions that do not talk to each other, a booking system they are thinking about switching, a phone that rings out at lunch, and no one to ask whether the AI tool a rep is pitching is real.

Not for: spas that want IT support. I do not fix the printer. I own the systems that run the business.

Questions
Do you only work with GoHighLevel?

No. I build on it and know it deeply, so if you run it or are considering it, that is an advantage. If you run something else that works, the job is to make it work better, not to migrate you.

Will you implement the changes or just recommend them?

Steph: your actual answer: inside the seat, or scoped separately.

What about HIPAA?

I designed Opteva to store no protected health information at all, which is the simplest way to stay out of trouble. For your stack, the rule is the same: know exactly what is stored where, keep clinical data in the clinical system, and never let a marketing tool touch it.
