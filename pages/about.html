Most business problems are not skill problems. They are systems problems. You are good at what you do. You are missing the infrastructure that makes it visible, consistent and repeatable when you are busy.

The operator years

I ran a med spa for over two decades. For most of that time I was doing the treatments, managing the team, handling the marketing and figuring out why clients were not rebooking. I lived every pain point operators talk about, usually on the same day.

What 26 years taught me

Most of what goes wrong in a spa is not a talent problem. The injector is good. The front desk cares. The owner works harder than anyone. What is missing is the system that makes it all run when the owner is in a treatment room: the schedule that fills itself, the rebooking that happens without a reminder, the numbers that arrive on time, the marketing that books instead of decorates.

I ranked my own company to the top of Google without an agency. I built the rebooking and follow-up that kept the book full. I set prices from margin, not from the spa down the street. None of that was software knowledge. It was operator knowledge, applied with discipline.

The builder years

After 26 years I spent a year deep in AI systems and built Opteva, a content platform for med spas and the agencies that serve them, on GoHighLevel. I built it because I had been the owner sending the draft back with "this isn't really us" and no notes. I knew the gap because I had lived inside it. More on Opteva here.

Building it taught me the fourth seat. Most owners are choosing booking systems, CRMs and AI tools from brochures and sales calls. I have shipped the software. That changes what I can tell you about yours.

Why a fractional practice

A single-location spa cannot afford a COO, a CFO, a CMO and a CTO. It also cannot afford to keep running without them, because the owner is doing all four jobs between patients. I take one or two of those seats, part time, with real ownership, for owners who want an operator in the room rather than a consultant on a call.

How I work

Three rules I do not break.

Operator first

I did not learn this from the outside. I ran a med spa for 26 years and did the work myself. Every recommendation has to survive a Tuesday afternoon with a full book.

Solutions, not suggestions

I am not selling a deck. I take ownership of a seat and the outcomes in it. There is a real difference between advice and an operator, and most owners have only met the first one.

Show, do not tell

I ranked my own company to #1 on Google without an agency and built a platform instead of recommending one. I teach what I have done, not what I have read about.

What drives me

The parts of this I genuinely love.

  • Closing the gaps nobody talks about. Every operator I have met has gaps they do not know exist. I am obsessed with finding them and fixing the one that costs the most first.
  • Systems that work without you. The best system runs while you are with a patient. I love building the thing that gives an owner their evenings back.
  • Craft still matters. Aesthetic hands are as important as aesthetic eyes. I spent 26 years caring about how the work was done, not just that it got done. That instinct moved into the business side with me.
  • Making the complex simple. Finance and technology intimidate people because the people explaining them make them complicated. My job is to make them clear enough that there is no reason not to act.
