In my spaI ran the provider schedule, hired and reviewed the team, built the rebooking that kept the book full, and wrote the front-desk script myself. Twenty-six years of Tuesdays. Steph: one specific operations story with a number: utilization, rebooking rate, no-show rate, a hiring turnaround.

What the seat covers

  • Provider schedules and utilization. Who works when, which rooms, which services, and how full the book actually is against capacity.
  • Front desk and phones. How calls are answered, how consults are booked, what happens to a missed call, how the room turns over.
  • Rebooking and retention. The system that gets a patient back on the schedule before they leave, and the follow-up when they do not.
  • SOPs and training. The written version of how the spa works, so it runs the same on the day you are not there.
  • Hiring, onboarding and reviews. Job descriptions, interview structure, 30-60-90 plans, compensation reviews that keep good providers.
  • Vendors and inventory. Product ordering, par levels, device service contracts, the supplier relationships nobody owns.
  • Compliance in the day-to-day. Consent workflows, photo handling, charting discipline, the things that are fine until they are not.

What you get each month

  • A weekly operations review: utilization, rebooking, no-shows, open roles, the three things to fix this week.
  • Ownership of the fixes, not a list of them. If the front desk script needs rewriting, I rewrite it and train it.
  • One SOP or system built or repaired each month, in priority order from the audit.
  • A monthly summary for you in plain English: what moved, what did not, what is next.
  • Steph: anything else you commit to monthly.

How an engagement runs

  1. The audit first. Two to three weeks across all four areas, so the seat starts with a ranked list, not a guess.
  2. Thirty-day setup. Access, baselines, the first dashboard, the first fixes that pay for the month.
  3. The cadence. Steph: days or hours per month, remote or on site, how the month is structured.
  4. Term and price. Steph: minimum term and whether the price is public or scoped after the audit.

Who this is for

Owner-operators of a single-location spa doing one to five million who are still treating patients and still running the building from memory. You have a team. You may have a manager. What you do not have is anyone who owns how the whole thing runs.

Not for: spas without a team yet, or owners who want the plan but will not change how the front desk answers the phone. The seat only works when I can change things.

Questions
I already have an office manager. Why would I need this?

A manager runs the day. A COO runs the system behind the day and holds the manager to a plan. Most good managers get better with someone to report to who understands the work, and that person is usually not the owner between patients.

Will you be on site?

Steph: your actual answer.

How do I know it is working?

Three numbers you can read weekly: utilization, rebooking rate, and no-show rate. If they are not moving inside ninety days, we talk about why, and the answer may be that this was the wrong seat.
