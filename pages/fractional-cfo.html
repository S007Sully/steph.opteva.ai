In my spaI set the prices, paid the providers, bought the devices and read the P&L every month for 26 years. I know which services carry the margin and which ones only look busy. Steph: one specific finance story with a number: a pricing change, a margin fix, a membership result, a cash crunch you got through.

What the seat covers

  • Cash flow forecast. Thirteen weeks out, updated weekly, so payroll and device payments stop being a surprise.
  • Margin by service line. What each treatment actually earns after product, provider pay and room time. Usually the first shock.
  • Pricing. Set from margin and capacity, not from the spa down the street. Reviewed on a schedule, not when cash gets tight.
  • Provider compensation. Commission structures that reward the behavior you want and still leave margin in the room.
  • Memberships and packages. Honest economics: what a member is worth over a year and whether the structure is helping or hiding a discount.
  • The KPI dashboard. Eight to ten numbers you read every week, built from your booking system and your books.
  • Bank and lender readiness. The reporting a lender wants to see before a line of credit, a second location or an exit.
  • Working with your bookkeeper and CPA. They close the books and file the taxes. I make sure what they produce is on time, correct and used.

What you get each month

  • A monthly financial review in plain English: where the money went, where it is going, what to change.
  • The weekly dashboard, maintained, with the three numbers that matter flagged.
  • One pricing, compensation or membership decision worked through each month, with the math shown.
  • The cash forecast, updated, with the dates that need attention.
  • Steph: tools you report in (QuickBooks, spreadsheets, a dashboard you built), and anything else you commit to monthly.

How an engagement runs

  1. The audit first. Two to three weeks across all four areas, so the seat starts with a ranked list, not a guess.
  2. Thirty-day setup. Access, baselines, the first dashboard, the first fixes that pay for the month.
  3. The cadence. Steph: days or hours per month, remote or on site, how the month is structured.
  4. Term and price. Steph: minimum term and whether the price is public or scoped after the audit.

Who this is for

Owners whose revenue has grown and whose take-home has not. You have a bookkeeper. You get a P&L six weeks late and do not quite trust it. You set prices years ago and have not had the time or the numbers to revisit them.

Not for: spas without clean books at all. If the bookkeeping is a year behind, that gets fixed first, and I will tell you who to call.

Questions
Are you a bookkeeper or an accountant?

Neither. Your bookkeeper records what happened and your CPA files the return. A CFO decides what should happen next, and makes sure the numbers that drive that decision show up on time. I work alongside both.

What if I do not want to raise prices?

Then we will not. Pricing is one lever of several. Margin by service, provider pay structure, membership design and capacity all move take-home without touching the menu.

How fast will I see a difference?

The dashboard and the cash forecast exist inside thirty days, and they change how you make decisions immediately. The margin and pricing work usually shows in the P&L inside a quarter.
